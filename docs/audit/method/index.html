Seven days, and four of them are reading.

An audit is mostly boring work done carefully. Here is the actual schedule, so you can judge whether the fee is fair.

DayWhat happensWhat you get
0You send the export and a sentence about what annoys you. We confirm scope or decline.A yes or a no within one business day.
1Normalise the file: deduplicate, catch the tools paid annually, find the ones hiding in a card statement rather than an invoice.A cost inventory you probably do not have.
2For each line, find the two or three realistic replacements. Reject the ones that fail on licences, data residency, or missing features.A shortlist per line, with rejects written down.
3Price the shortlist properly: hosting, backup, monitoring, and the hours to migrate. Not the licence fee alone.The real annual cost of each path.
4Sequencing. Which migration blocks another, which ones break mid-quarter, which need a freeze window.An order of operations.
5Write the document. Every recommendation has a number attached and a reason it is safe.Spreadsheet and PDF.
6The call. You push back, we revise or defend.A document you can act on.

What we need from you

  • A CSV or PDF export of software charges covering twelve months.
  • Headcount, and roughly how many people use each tool.
  • Anything you know is contractual: annual prepay, minimum terms, procurement rules.
  • One person who can answer questions in a few hours, not days.

That is it. No infrastructure access, no repository, no admin credentials. An audit of spend does not require access to your systems, and asking for it would be a warning sign.

What we will not do

Worth being explicit, because scope creep in both directions is how these engagements go wrong.

  • We do not perform the migration. The document tells you what to move and in what order. Doing it is a separate project, and often better done by whoever already knows your systems.
  • We do not negotiate with vendors. This is not procurement consultancy and we hold no leverage with your suppliers.
  • We do not manage anything afterwards. There is no retainer, no monitoring, no monthly fee.
  • We do not guarantee savings. If the honest answer is that you are already lean, that is what the document will say, and the guarantee on the pricing page exists because of it.

Why self-hosting gets a line item

Every "free" replacement in this industry is free the way a puppy is free. Someone patches it, someone restarts it when it falls over, someone gets paged at 2am.

We put a flat $900 a year per replacement against hosting and upkeep. For a small tool that is generous. For a database it is optimistic. The point is that the number appears in the same column as the subscription it replaces, so the comparison is honest rather than flattering.

When a replacement cannot beat its subscription once that line is included, it gets marked "keep" and we move on. Roughly a third of what most companies pay for survives that test.
