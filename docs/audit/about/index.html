A small operation with a public dataset.

No team page, no stock photography, no claimed client logos. Here is what actually exists behind this site.

This audit service grew out of an open source alternatives directory: 49 commercial products mapped to 101 open source projects, with stars, licences and languages pulled from the GitHub API at build time.

The directory answers "what could replace this". The audit answers the harder question: "what should we replace, in what order, and what does it actually cost once someone has to run it".

The second question needs your invoices, which is why it is a paid engagement and not a website.

What we are good at

  • Reading a software bill and spotting the lines nobody has questioned in three years.
  • Knowing which open source projects are actually maintained, and which are abandoned repositories with a nice README.
  • Pricing the unglamorous parts: hosting, backups, patching, and the hours to migrate.
  • Saying "keep that one" when it is the right answer.

What we are not

  • Not a migration shop. We do not implement.
  • Not procurement. No vendor relationships, no negotiating leverage.
  • Not a compliance vendor. If you need SOC 2 paperwork and a security questionnaire, this is the wrong supplier.
  • Not a research firm. The percentages on the calculator are a rule of thumb, not a benchmark from hundreds of clients. We would rather say that than invent a sample size.

Why the site looks like a ledger

Because the deliverable is a ledger. The page you are reading is the same document structure you get: lines, annual figures, a replacement, and a subtraction. Designing it as anything else would be dishonest about what the work is.

The green bar behind the text is the paper accounting software replaced. It seemed more appropriate than another dark-mode dashboard.
