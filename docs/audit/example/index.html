Line by line

The part a spreadsheet alone will not tell you.

Figma Professional
Twenty-four editors at $15. Penpot covers design and prototype handoff, and the files stay on your server.
Jira Standard
Thirty users at $8.15. Plane's cycles and modules map onto how the team already works.
Confluence Standard
Thirty users at $6.40. Outline is faster and the search actually finds things.
Slack Pro
Kept. Every replacement here costs more in lost habits than it saves, and the network effect is real.
Auth0
Five thousand monthly active users. Keycloak is dull software, which is what you want under login.
Mixpanel Growth
Event volume is the cost driver. PostHog self-hosted also brings replays, which were being paid for separately.
1Password Business
Kept. Security tooling is the last place to optimise on price, and the migration risk is not worth $2,876.

What we would do first

Confluence first. Lowest risk of the four: nobody's workflow depends on the wiki's specific software, and Outline imports the content. A week of an intern's time.

Auth0 second. Keycloak is the least pleasant migration here, but it sits under everything, so leaving it until later means doing the work twice.

Figma and Jira last. Both are habit-heavy. Designers and engineers will resist, and resistance is expensive. Run them in parallel with the team's own champions, not as an IT project.

Mixpanel whenever the contract renews. It is the biggest single saving and the least urgent, because event volume only grows.

The caveat on all of this

This is a modelled example built from list prices, not a rescued client. It shows the method and the arithmetic. Your numbers will differ in both directions, and the audit exists to replace this with your actual invoices.

Two things move the totals most: seat count, and whether you already pay annually. A team of twelve with three tools has very little to recover, and we will say so before taking the fee.

Check the prices yourself

Every figure above is public list pricing at the time of writing. If you think a number is wrong, check it — and tell us, because a wrong price in a public example is worse for us than for you.
