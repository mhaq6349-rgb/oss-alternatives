The questions worth asking before you pay anyone.

Including the ones that make us look worse.

Who actually does the work?

A small operation. The same person who built the alternatives directory behind this site reads your invoices and writes the document. There is no account manager and no offshore team.

That is a limitation as well as a selling point: we take a handful of audits at a time, and we will tell you when the next slot is rather than pretend otherwise.

Do you take a cut of the savings?

No. The fee is flat. A percentage would pay us to recommend the riskiest migration with the biggest headline number, and to stay quiet when a replacement costs more to run than it saves.

What if you find nothing worth changing?

You get a document saying so, with the analysis. Half the fee is refunded under the guarantee because we did not clear the three-times threshold. A thin stack is a good problem to have.

Is my financial data safe?

You send invoices, not credentials. We ask for card numbers to be redacted, we do not need access to any system, and the working files are deleted after delivery unless you ask us to keep them.

If your policy requires an NDA before sharing anything, say so in the first message and we will send one back.

Do you do the migration?

No. The document tells you what to move and in what order. Most migrations are better done by the people who already know the systems, and we would be learning your infrastructure at your expense.

What about licences like AGPL?

They matter, and they are part of the shortlist. Some projects carry modified licences — several of the tools in our directory are marked "custom licence" because GitHub cannot identify them. If a licence creates an obligation your business cannot accept, the option is rejected and we say why.

Why do you count hosting as a cost?

Because someone has to run it. A flat $900 a year per replacement covers a server, backups and the hours to patch it. For a database that number is optimistic, and the document says when it is.

Can you audit just one tool?

No, and it would not be worth $900. The value is in seeing the whole stack at once: the replacement for one line is sometimes the tool you already pay for on another.

We are on annual contracts. Does that matter?

It changes the sequencing, not the analysis. You get a plan keyed to your renewal dates so you can give notice in time, rather than discovering the deadline after it passed.

How do I start?

Send a short message with your headcount, approximate annual software spend, and what you suspect is wasteful. If it looks like a fit we send a scope question and an invoice. If it does not, we say so.
